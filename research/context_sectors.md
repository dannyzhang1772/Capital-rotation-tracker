# Context sectors: Financials, Utilities, Materials and Communication Services (Media)

*Research note for the Capital Rotation Tracker (sector ids `financials`, `utilities`, `materials`, `communication_media`). Tracked at sector level with lighter sub-industry depth; they are where capital goes when it leaves technology, and vice versa. Prepared 7 November 2026.*

## Financials

Financials trade on the yield curve, credit and regulation, and they were the epicentre of the one regime technology was not. The credit crunch began with the Fed's 50 bp cut on 18 September 2007; by February 2008 analysts expected financial profits to fall 23%; Lehman filed on 15 September 2008 and AIG fell 61% that day. The sector lost 82.6% from the October 2007 peak to 9 March 2009 and about 57% in calendar 2008. The May 2009 stress tests (10 of 19 banks needing $74.6 billion) ended the panic, and Dodd-Frank (21 July 2010) set the frame for a decade: Volcker Rule, Durbin interchange caps, the CFPB and stress testing, which capped returns and pushed activity toward non-banks and payment networks.

The 2010s were rate-driven rotations: worst sector in 2011 (-17.1%) on the US downgrade and Eurozone crisis, best in 2012 (+28.8%) on QE3 and housing. The 2016 election produced the best bank month on record (KBE +18.7% in November 2016) and the May 2018 rollback of enhanced supervision to $250 billion delivered part of that promise. COVID brought dividend caps and a buyback ban (25 June 2020) and a -1.7% year; 2021's reflation (+35.0%) reversed it. Silicon Valley Bank's failure on 10 March 2023 (KRE -14.4% in three sessions, First Republic -62% in a day) was the first deposit run of the smartphone era and ended with the BTFP and JPMorgan's purchase of First Republic.

Three structural changes followed. GICS moved Visa, Mastercard, PayPal and Fiserv into Financials on 17 March 2023 (+2.7 points of index weight). The 2025 deregulation cycle (eSLR proposal 25 June 2025, finalised 25 November; Basel III endgame re-proposed with reductions on 19 March 2026; Capital One allowed to buy Discover) and the GENIUS Act of 18 July 2025 reopened bank M&A and made stablecoins a bank product. And credit stress returned outside the banks: First Brands' September 2025 bankruptcy exposed private-credit and trade-finance losses, and in October-November 2026 CoreWeave's bonds went distressed and hyperscaler CDS hit their widest since 2021. The Fed's 16 September 2026 hike helps margins, but XLF is up only 6.3% year-to-date against 40.6% for energy.

## Utilities

Utilities are the market's bond proxy; their relative performance mirrors the 10-year yield. They fell 45.9% in the GFC bear (less than the index) and led whenever yields collapsed: best sector of 2011 (+19.9%), +29.0% in 2014, +16.3% in 2016. The May 2013 taper tantrum was the defining risk event: -3.4% in the first week against -1.07% for the S&P 500 and +13.2% for the year versus +32.4%. PG&E's Camp Fire collapse (-52% on 14 January 2019, Chapter 11 on 29 January) added wildfire liability as a structural discount for Western utilities.

The story was rewritten twice after 2022. Utilities returned +1.6% in 2022 while the index fell 18.1%, then were the worst sector of 2023 (-7.1%) as the Fed's final hike pushed long yields to cycle highs. Then the AI-power narrative arrived: Talen's March 2024 campus sale to Amazon, Constellation's Three Mile Island restart for Microsoft (20 September 2024, +14% intraday) and Goldman's forecast that data centers would be the largest driver of US power demand through 2030 made utilities the top sector for much of 2024 (+23.4% for the year), led by Vistra (+108% year-to-date by September) and Constellation (+60%), with PJM capacity costs rising to more than $16 billion from $2.2 billion.

2026 turned the trade political. New York suspended large data-center permits for a year on 14 July 2026, Chicago, Pennsylvania and New Jersey followed, at least 47 large projects were delayed or denied, and Gallup found 71% of Americans opposed to local data centers. After the 3 November Democratic sweep, Goldman estimated moratoria could affect 45% of planned capacity. With the 10-year up about a point from its February low and the Fed hiking, XLU is up only 3.5% year-to-date through 5 November 2026.

## Materials

Materials trade on China, the dollar and the commodity cycle, with gold miners a separate real-yield and fear trade. The 2008 crash was total: oil from $147 on 11 July 2008 to $30 by December, the sector -59.6% peak-to-trough. China's stimulus produced the supercycle top of early 2011 (copper above $10,000 a tonne), after which the sector returned -9.6% in 2011 and declined into the 2015 bust (China devaluation, oil below $30, the Glencore debt scare; materials -8.4%). The 2016 reflation (+16.7%) and 2021 reopening (+27.3%) were the two cyclical rallies; 2018 (-14.7%) showed tariffs help steelmakers but hurt the sector when they slow global growth; 2024 (0.0%) was a dead year as China's property slump offset copper.

Policy has dominated since 2025: steel and aluminum tariffs doubled to 50% on 4 June 2025, a 50% copper tariff (9 July 2025) sent Comex copper to a record $5.65 a pound, and the April 2026 proclamation restructured the metals tariffs on a full-value basis. The state took direct equity in MP Materials on 10 July 2025 (shares +50%), the template later used for Intel. Gold was the standout: past $4,000 in October 2025, near $4,350 at year-end (+60%, best year since at least 1988), GDX up roughly 115-134%; the Iran war pushed gold to about $5,400 in March 2026 before a 21% Q2 drawdown in miners as yields rose. Lithium went the other way: Albemarle lost $511 million in 2025 and idled Kemerton in February 2026. XLB is up 16.9% year-to-date in 2026, behind energy but far ahead of technology.

## Communication Services (Media and Entertainment)

The sector did not exist until 28 September 2018, when GICS folded Alphabet, Facebook, Netflix and Comcast in with the carriers and media groups. Before that, media was a story of capital leaving legacy distribution: Google's $1.65 billion YouTube purchase (October 2006) and Comcast's $30 billion NBC Universal deal (December 2009) bracketed the shift of advertising toward platforms, pay-TV households peaked in 2011 and cord-cutters became the majority in 2022. Disney+ (12 November 2019, 10 million day-one sign-ups) briefly re-rated legacy media as growth, until Netflix's first subscriber loss (19 April 2022, -35% in a day) ended the "growth at any cost" era and the sector finished 2022 at -39.9%, the worst in the index.

Then the best and the worst years in its short history. Meta's "year of efficiency" (+150% in 2023), Netflix's ad tier and Alphabet's AI narrative drove returns of 55.8% in 2023, 40.2% in 2024 and 33.6% in 2025, the top sector in both 2024 and 2025. Legacy media consolidated: Skydance closed its Paramount merger on 7 August 2025, WBD jumped 33% on Paramount's approach in September 2025, EA agreed a record $55 billion buyout on 29 September 2025, and Paramount outbid Netflix for WBD at $110 billion enterprise value on 27 February 2026 (closed 6 October 2026). In 2026 the growth composition became a liability: Alphabet fell 7% on 23 July on a $205 billion capex guide and 13% ($490 billion) on 29 October after a second quarter of negative free cash flow, and XLC is the worst sector of 2026 at -10.6% year-to-date.

## What the sectors trade on today

**Financials.** The curve after a Fed hiking again (3.75-4% since 16 September 2026), the pace of capital relief (eSLR final, Basel re-proposal), M&A approval, and credit: private-credit exposures, AI-infrastructure lending and the widest hyperscaler CDS since 2021. Banks gain from higher short rates and deregulation; capital markets and insurers carry the AI credit cycle and long yields at multi-year highs. Stablecoin economics (GENIUS Act, Circle, Coinbase) are the growth sub-plot.

**Utilities.** The 10-year yield first, then the political durability of data-center load growth: state moratoria, PJM capacity prices and affordability politics under a Democratic Congress, wildfire liability and allowed ROEs. Regulated names trade as bond proxies again; independent power producers trade with the AI complex and sold off with it in October-November 2026.

**Materials.** Gold miners trade on real yields, central-bank buying and geopolitics, with record free cash flow at $4,400 gold and sensitivity to every Fed signal. Metals and mining trade on the Section 232 regime, copper's grid and data-center demand, and China. Chemicals trade on natural-gas feedstock after the Hormuz shock, destocking and industrial production, with 50% metals tariffs raising downstream costs.

**Communication Services.** Capex intensity and free cash flow at Alphabet and Meta, search disruption and antitrust appeals, the ad cycle, streaming profitability and sports-rights inflation, and the integration of Paramount-WBD under heavy debt. The sector is the first casualty of AI-capex doubt because its two largest members are also two of the largest spenders.
