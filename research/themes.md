# Themes as overlays: AI, hyperscalers, data-center power, GLP-1, reshoring, cybersecurity, nuclear

*Research note for the Capital Rotation Tracker (theme ids `ai`, `hyperscalers`, `datacenter_power`, `glp1`, `reshoring_infrastructure`, `cybersecurity`, `nuclear`). Themes overlap sector buckets; names also sit in their home sub-industries. Prepared 7 November 2026.*

## Why overlays

Sector buckets say where a company is filed; themes say why its stock moved. Since 2022 the largest flows have followed cross-sector narratives rather than GICS lines: the AI trade spans semiconductors, utilities, industrials and REITs; GLP-1 moves food, devices and telehealth as well as pharma.

## AI beneficiaries (from 30 November 2022)

ChatGPT, Microsoft's $10 billion OpenAI investment (23 January 2023) and Nvidia's $11 billion guidance (24 May 2023, stock +26%) created the theme; the Magnificent Seven rose about 76% cap-weighted in 2023. Its shocks have been about cost and financing, not demand: DeepSeek (27 January 2025, Nvidia -17%, a record $589 billion loss) questioned how much compute frontier models need; the September-October 2025 Nvidia-OpenAI ($100 billion), AMD-OpenAI (6 GW plus warrants) and Oracle ($300 billion backlog) deals raised the "circular financing" question; and in October-November 2026 CoreWeave's bonds fell to 78 cents, its CDS passed 1,000 bp, Gartner put more than $4 trillion of data-center assets at write-down risk and the Democratic midterm sweep raised moratorium risk. The overlay now trades on credit (neocloud and Oracle spreads, hyperscaler free cash flow) and on whether monetised AI revenue catches up with capex.

## Hyperscalers / cloud capex (from 2011)

Combined capex of Alphabet, Amazon, Meta, Microsoft and Oracle rose from $37.8 billion in Q1 2023 to $140.6 billion in Q4 2025; 2026 guidance clusters around $730 billion. The market's rule since Amazon's October 2025 print (AWS +20.2%, stock +13%) and Alphabet's 2026 shocks (-7% in July, -13% in October) is simple: capex funded by cash flow and matched by cloud acceleration is rewarded; capex funded by debt (Alphabet's long-term debt +111% in six months, $296 billion of hyperscaler issuance in 2026) is punished. It trades on cloud growth, free cash flow, bond issuance and GPU depreciation schedules.

## Data-center power and cooling (from 1 January 2023)

Vertiv's 252% rise in 2023, GE Vernova's April 2024 spin-off, Talen's $650 million Amazon campus sale (March 2024) and Constellation's Three Mile Island restart (20 September 2024) made power, cooling and gas turbines the industrial expression of AI. PJM capacity costs rose to more than $16 billion from $2.2 billion, which is also the theme's problem: electricity bills produced a 2026 backlash (New York's moratorium of 14 July, Chicago, Pennsylvania, New Jersey, 71% public opposition) and Goldman's estimate that 45% of planned capacity faces moratorium risk after the midterms. It trades on contracted PPAs, turbine and transformer lead times, and state politics.

## GLP-1 / obesity (from 1 June 2022)

Wegovy's 2021 approval and the SELECT cardiovascular result (8 August 2023, Novo +18%) turned obesity drugs into a cardiometabolic franchise and a short thesis for food, devices and dialysis. The theme then became a share war: Lilly's Zepbound (November 2023) took share, Novo cut guidance on 29 July 2025 (-22% in a day, -47% for the year), the November 2025 TrumpRx deal set $245 and $50 monthly prices with a tariff exemption, and Lilly became the first $1 trillion health-care company on 21 November 2025. It trades on oral GLP-1 launches, pricing and reimbursement, compounding and telehealth volumes.

## Reshoring and infrastructure (from 15 November 2021)

Three laws built the theme: the IIJA (15 November 2021, $550 billion new spending), the CHIPS Act (9 August 2022, $52.7 billion plus a 25% fab credit) and the IRA (16 August 2022, $391 billion). The One Big Beautiful Bill Act (4 July 2025) split it: wind, solar and EV credits were phased out while manufacturing, nuclear and factory-depreciation incentives survived. Tariffs now do the work subsidies did: 50% metals duties, the January 2026 chip tariff with offsets for US fabs, Taiwan's $250 billion pledge and Micron's $250 billion US plan. It trades on fab and data-center construction backlogs, grid orders, tariff design after the Supreme Court ruling and whether moratoria slow the non-residential pipeline.

## Cybersecurity (from 11 November 2014)

Sony (November 2014), Equifax (September 2017), SolarWinds (December 2020) and Colonial Pipeline (May 2021, Executive Order 14028) made security a non-discretionary budget line and a policy mandate. The 19 July 2024 CrowdStrike outage (8.5 million devices, stock -11%) moved share between platforms without cutting budgets, and 2025's consolidation (Google-Wiz $32 billion, Palo Alto-CyberArk $25 billion) set takeover premiums. It trades on platform consolidation, federal spending, breach headlines and, since April 2026, whether AI agents expand the attack surface or compress seat-based security SaaS.

## Nuclear renaissance (from 1 June 2023)

Fukushima (11 March 2011) halved uranium prices and began a decade-long bear market; the turn came from hyperscalers: Three Mile Island (September 2024), the Google-Kairos and Amazon-X-energy SMR deals (October 2024), Trump's 23 May 2025 executive orders (10 large reactors under construction by 2030), the $80 billion Westinghouse program (28 October 2025), X-energy's record $1 billion IPO (24 April 2026) and Westinghouse's S-1 (31 July 2026). It trades on uranium and enrichment prices, DOE and NRC milestones, hyperscaler PPAs and the same data-center politics as the power theme; existing fleets carry the cash flow, SMR developers the option value.
