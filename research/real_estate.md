# Real Estate: where the capital went, 1999-2026

*FTSE Nareit total returns; sources in `events_group1.json`.*

## Preamble: 1999-2005

REITs were the anti-Nasdaq. Shunned in 1998-99, they absorbed the rotation out of growth when the bubble burst: +26% in 2000, +14% in 2001, +4% in 2002 against three down years for the S&P 500, with mortgage REITs +77% in 2001 as the Fed cut and the curve steepened. The 2003-06 bull market compounded prices 99% in three years on cheap debt, CMBS growth and private-equity takeovers, ending with Blackstone's $39bn Equity Office buyout in February 2007; 2006 alone returned 35%, with office, health care, self storage and apartments all above 40%. The warning was 2005, when seventeen Fed hikes flattened the curve and mortgage REITs lost 23% (Annaly -41%) while equity REITs rose 12%: the mortgage sub-sector trades on the yield curve, not on property.

## 2007-2009: crash and recapitalization

Prices peaked in February 2007. Subprime failures and the August 2007 money-market seizure closed the CMBS market; REITs lost 16% in 2007 and mortgage REITs 42%, then 38% in 2008 as Lehman raised solvency fears for every leveraged landlord. Dispersion was enormous: industrial REITs -67%, lodging -60%, regional malls -61% (General Growth filed the largest REIT bankruptcy on April 16, 2009), commercial mortgage REITs -75%. Self storage was the only positive sector (+5%). The bottom was made by equity: starting with Simon in March 2009, REITs raised $37.5bn in 91 secondary offerings, and the most distressed groups led the 28% rebound (lodging +67%, malls +63%, SL Green +102%).

## 2010-2016: the QE yield trade and its tantrum

With the 10-year falling from 3.3% to 1.8% over 2010-12, REITs became the retail bond substitute: +28%, +8% and +20%. Apartments led (+47% in 2010) as homeownership fell, self storage returned 35% in 2011, and 2012 brought the first "new economy" REITs when American Tower and Equinix converted (Equinix +103%).

Bernanke's May 22, 2013 taper remarks exposed the duration risk: mortgage REITs fell 21% in six weeks and Annaly cut its dividend from $0.45 to $0.30, net lease, health care and apartments went negative, and REITs returned 3% in a year the index gained 32%. The 2014 reversal, when yields fell anyway, gave REITs 28% (apartments +40%, health care +33%). HCP's $1.3bn of impairments on skilled-nursing tenant HCR ManorCare (2015-16) pushed capital toward private-pay senior housing, and self storage's 41% 2015 triggered a development wave that left it flat for three years. The 10-year's record 1.36% in July 2016 and the GICS sector launch on September 1, 2016 marked the top of the bond-proxy trade; it reversed the week Trump was elected.

## 2017-2019: retail apocalypse, logistics boom, Fed pivot

E-commerce moved capital from one REIT sector to another. Store closures ran at 7,000 a year from 2017, shopping centers lost 11% and 15% in 2017-18 and malls fell three years running. Industrial REITs returned 31%, 21% and 49% in 2016-19, data centers 44% in 2019 and towers 42% on 5G; by 2019 those groups were about a third of REIT market cap. The Powell pivot gave all REITs 29% in 2019.

## 2020-2021: COVID and the best year on record

COVID produced the widest dispersion in REIT history. Mortgage REITs lost 56% in Q1 2020 on repo margin calls, Host suspended its dividend, lodging finished -24%, malls -37%, office -18%, apartments -15%; data centers (+21%), industrial (+12%) and self storage (+13%) were the only positive sectors. The November 9, 2020 vaccine news began the best REIT year ever: +41% in 2021, with malls +92% (Simon +96%), shopping centers +65%, self storage +79%, apartments +64% on record rent growth and industrial +62% (Prologis +72%); office (+22%) and lodging (+18%) lagged.

## 2022-2023: rate shock and the regional-bank office collapse

The 10-year rose 243bp in 2022 and REITs lost 25%, the worst year since 2008: office -38%, apartments -32%, self storage -27%, mortgage REITs -27%, with only net lease (-6.5%) holding up. Two narrative drawdowns compounded it: Amazon's April 2022 admission of excess warehouse space ended the logistics premium (Prologis -31%) and Jim Chanos's short of "legacy" data centers took Digital Realty down 41%.

Silicon Valley Bank's failure on March 10, 2023 hit the banks that make 70% of CRE loans: SL Green fell 37% in a month, Vornado 27%, office returned 2% in a year all REITs returned 11%, and brokers' transaction revenue halved as global CRE investment fell 48%. Medical Properties Trust's Steward saga (-82% from peak) was health care's own credit event.

## 2023-2025: AI landlords, senior housing, a muted market

The Chanos thesis reversed within a year: AI leasing (Digital Realty signed more than $520m in Q3 2024) made data centers the top sector in 2023 (+30%) and 2024 (+25%), while towers lagged three years on carrier capex cuts and Sprint churn (telecom -14% in 2024; Crown Castle sold its fiber unit and cut its dividend 32% in March 2025). Health care became the structural winner: the 80-plus wave and zero new senior-housing supply gave Welltower 14% same-store NOI growth and the sector 14%, 24% and 28.5% in 2023-25.

The rest was rate-driven and muted. The September 2024 cut produced the best quarter since 2009 (+16.8%, Realty Income from $50 to $65) and the post-election yield rebound took it back (-8% in Q4); REITs returned 5% in 2024 and 2% in 2025. Beneath the index: the Sunbelt supply wave turned apartment rents negative (-9% in 2025), office bounced 21.5% in 2024 on return-to-office and lost 14% in 2025, self storage fell 10% as housing turnover hit 30-year lows, industrial REITs hit five-year lows after the Liberation Day tariffs before finishing +17%, data centers were the worst sector of 2025 (-14%) and agency mortgage REITs the best (+26%). In services, the NAR settlement knocked 10-14% off Zillow and Compass in a day, Opendoor became a meme stock and Compass bought Anywhere for $1.6bn.

## 2026: a good REIT year with a rate scare

REITs returned 14.9% in the first half and are up about 13% year to date, beating a market whose AI rally lost steam. Leadership is fundamental: lodging +57% through October on record travel and no supply, data centers +28-33% on Digital Realty's record 340 MW quarter, health care +19% (Welltower the only sector above NAV), self storage +10% and malls +13-29% (Simon's best rent growth in a decade). Iran-war inflation then forced the Fed's first hike since 2023 on September 16; the 10-year hit 5.10% on September 23, every property type fell in September (net lease -10.6%, mortgage REITs -13.7%) and 30-year mortgages reached 7.19%. October's 4.7% rebound came with a Fed pause and an 18% jump in towers when SpaceX-cellular fears reversed. The laggards are office (-11% through September), residential (negative for the year) and mortgage REITs (-12%).

## What the sub-industries trade on today

**Industrial and logistics REITs.** Tariff and import headlines versus reshoring and data-center land demand; new supply has collapsed, so mark-to-market rent spreads drive earnings. Prologis is up only 3% in 2026 after +25% in 2025 and remains rate-sensitive.

**Data center and tower REITs.** Two assets. Data centers trade on hyperscale leasing, power availability and the self-build threat at 25-30x FFO; towers on carrier capex, leverage and now satellite-network risk, still below 2021 highs.

**Residential REITs.** Supply. Sunbelt deliveries are peaking and coastal landlords outperform; the sector is negative for 2026 and waits for 2027 rent growth as starts have collapsed. Rent-control politics and 7% mortgages are the secondary drivers.

**Office and life-science REITs.** CMBS maturities, regional-bank lending, utilization data and conversions. The 2024 squeeze showed how fast the group moves on rates, but it is the worst sector of 2026 and trades on balance sheets and trophy leasing.

**Retail REITs.** Zero new supply, record occupancy and re-leasing spreads from tenant failures; malls have gone from pariah to core holding. The risk is a consumer slowdown under $4-plus gasoline.

**Net lease and specialty REITs.** The purest bond proxies: Realty Income and peers trade on the 10-year against acquisition cap rates, which is why they fell hardest in September 2026. Tenant credit is the idiosyncratic risk.

**Health care REITs.** Senior-housing occupancy and NOI growth against the 80-plus wave, with minimal new supply; Welltower's all-in pivot is the template. Skilled-nursing and hospital landlords still trade on operator credit and Medicaid policy.

**Self-storage REITs.** Housing turnover and street rates; the 2026 rebound reflects stabilizing rates and consolidation after a two-year slump, but 7% mortgages cap it.

**Lodging and resort REITs.** RevPAR, group bookings and inbound travel; 2026's leader after five years of neglect, and the most cyclical group if higher fuel and rates eventually hit demand.

**Mortgage REITs.** The yield curve, MBS spreads and the Fed's balance sheet, with GSE reform the policy wildcard; they led in 2025 on cuts and lost 12% in 2026 on hikes.

**Real estate services and brokerage.** Existing-home sales near 30-year lows, mortgage rates, commission compression after the NAR settlement and consolidation; commercial brokers trade on transaction volumes, again exposed to 5% yields.
